L1-2 · Basics: copula, demonstratives, particles

日本語ユニバース · Globi Genki L1-12 · For native English speakers 1 / 5
L1 N5
X は Y です
X wa Y desu
X is Y (identity)
Form Noun は Noun です. Negative: ~では ありません
私は学生です。
Watashi wa gakusei desu.
I am a student.
EN tip は is the topic marker (pronounced 'wa', not 'ha'). です = formal copula. No equivalent in English — Japanese marks the subject of discourse rather than 'I/you'.
L1 N5
~の~
~no~
Connecting nouns (possessive / attributive)
Form Noun の Noun. Owner + の + object.
山田さんの本
Yamada-san no hon
Yamada's book
EN tip の is far more versatile than English 'of' or '-'s. Also used in compound nouns like 日本の歌 (Japanese songs).
L1 N5
~か (question)
~ka
Sentence-end question marker
Form Statement + か. No rising intonation needed in formal speech.
学生ですか？
Gakusei desu ka?
Are you a student?
EN tip Japanese doesn't invert word order for questions like English. Just add か at the end.
L1 N5
X も Y
X mo Y
X also / X too
Form Replaces は or を to add 'also'.
私も学生です。
Watashi mo gakusei desu.
I am also a student.
EN tip Particles like も REPLACE は/を, they don't add to them.
L1 N5
Numbers 1-10, age, time
ichi, ni, san...
Basic counting
Form Number + counter (e.g., 〜歳 sai for age, 〜時 ji for time).
二十歳です。
Hatachi desu.
I'm 20 years old.
EN tip 20 years old is irregularly read 'hatachi' (not 'nijussai').
L2 N5
これ / それ / あれ / どれ
kore / sore / are / dore
This / that / that-far / which (pronoun)
Form Standalone pronouns. これ=near speaker, それ=near listener, あれ=far from both, どれ=which.
これは何ですか？
Kore wa nan desu ka?
What is this?
EN tip Three-way distance, not two like English ('this/that'). Important to learn the spatial logic.
L2 N5
この / その / あの + Noun
kono / sono / ano + N
This/that + noun (attributive)
Form Used BEFORE a noun. Cannot stand alone.
この本は私のです。
Kono hon wa watashi no desu.
This book is mine.
EN tip Different from これ — これ stands alone, この must attach to a noun. Common mistake.
L2 N5
ここ / そこ / あそこ / どこ
koko / soko / asoko / doko
Here / there / over there / where
Form Location pronouns.
トイレはどこですか？
Toire wa doko desu ka?
Where is the toilet?
EN tip あそこ (asoko) means 'over there' (far from both speakers). No direct one-word English equivalent.
L2 N5
~ね / ~よ
~ne / ~yo
Sentence-end particles (agreement / new info)
Form ね = seeking agreement (right?). よ = telling listener something they don't know.
いいですね。 / 行きますよ。
Ii desu ne. / Ikimasu yo.
Nice, isn't it? / I'm going (FYI).
EN tip Carry social/emotional weight. Overusing よ can sound bossy. ね is friendly.
L2 N5
~じゃ ありません
~ja arimasen
Negation of nouns/な-adjectives
Form Replace です with じゃありません (casual: じゃない).
学生じゃありません。
Gakusei ja arimasen.
I'm not a student.
EN tip じゃ is the spoken contraction of では. Formal speech uses では ありません.
L2 N5
誰の N (whose N)
dare no N
Whose ~?
Form 誰 (who) + の + Noun.
誰のかばんですか？
Dare no kaban desu ka?
Whose bag is it?
EN tip Same の pattern as L1, just with 誰 (who).

L3-4 · Verbs, existence, time

日本語ユニバース · Globi Genki L1-12 · For native English speakers 2 / 5
L3 N5
Verb ます-form (polite present/future)
~masu
Polite present tense verb
Form Group 1 (う-verbs): 書く→書きます. Group 2 (る-verbs): 食べる→食べます. Irregular: する→します, 来る→きます.
本を読みます。
Hon o yomimasu.
I read a book.
EN tip ます form covers both present and future ('I read' AND 'I will read'). Context decides.
L3 N5
Object particle を
~o
Direct object marker
Form Direct object + を + transitive verb. Written を but pronounced 'o'.
コーヒーを飲みます。
Koohii o nomimasu.
I drink coffee.
EN tip Pronounced 'o' identical to お, but written differently. Only used for direct object.
L3 N5
Location particles で / に / へ
de / ni / e
Where action happens / destination
Form で = where action takes place. に = direction/specific time/destination. へ = direction (written へ, pronounced 'e').
学校で勉強します。
Gakkou de benkyou shimasu.
I study at school.
EN tip で vs に is a HUGE pitfall: で = activity location (study AT school), に = end-point (go TO school).
L3 N5
Frequency adverbs
yoku / tokidoki / amari / zenzen
often / sometimes / not much / not at all
Form Negative frequency (あまり, ぜんぜん) must end with negative verb.
あまり食べません。
Amari tabemasen.
I don't eat much.
EN tip あまり and ぜんぜん REQUIRE negative verbs. 'I rarely eat' = 'I don't eat much' in Japanese logic.
L3 N5
Word order — SOV
Subject-Object-Verb
Verb goes LAST
Form Subject は Object を Verb. Particles do the grammatical work, so word order is flexible.
私はパンを食べます。
Watashi wa pan o tabemasu.
I eat bread.
EN tip BIGGEST mental shift for English speakers. The verb always comes last. Train your brain to wait for the verb.
L4 N5
ある / いる (existence)
aru / iru
There is / there are
Form ある = inanimate (things). いる = animate (people, animals). Use が for subject.
猫がいます。 / 本があります。
Neko ga imasu. / Hon ga arimasu.
There is a cat. / There is a book.
EN tip Choosing aru/iru by animacy is alien to English. Plants and most insects use ある.
L4 N5
Past tense (ました / でした)
~mashita / ~deshita
Polite past tense
Form ます → ました (verbs). です → でした (nouns/な-adj). Negative past: ~ませんでした.
昨日学校へ行きました。
Kinou gakkou e ikimashita.
I went to school yesterday.
EN tip Past tense affixes are very regular — much easier than English irregular verbs.
L4 N5
Location nouns (上下中前後)
ue / shita / naka / mae / ushiro
Above / under / inside / front / back
Form Noun の + location word + に. Sequence: thing-の-location-に.
机の上にあります。
Tsukue no ue ni arimasu.
It's on top of the desk.
EN tip Spatial relations expressed as nouns, not prepositions. 'On the desk' = 'at the desk's top'.
L4 N5
Time particle に / で
~ni / ~de
At a specific time / time-window
Form に for specific clock time, day, year. No に for relative time (今日, 明日).
七時に起きます。
Shichi-ji ni okimasu.
I get up at 7 o'clock.
EN tip Don't add に to 今日, 明日, 昨日 — they already imply time. Common mistake.
L4 N5
~と (with someone)
~to
Together with
Form Person/thing + と. Different から と (and so).
友達と映画を見ます。
Tomodachi to eiga o mimasu.
I watch a movie with a friend.
EN tip Same particle と does both 'with' AND 'and' (for lists). Context distinguishes.

L5-6 · Adjectives, te-form ★

日本語ユニバース · Globi Genki L1-12 · For native English speakers 3 / 5
L5 N5
い-adjectives
i-adjectives
True adjectives (conjugate)
Form End in い: 高い, 安い, 大きい. Negative: 高くない. Past: 高かった. Negative past: 高くなかった.
この本は高いです。
Kono hon wa takai desu.
This book is expensive.
EN tip い-adj. conjugate themselves like verbs (carry tense). NEVER add です to make past — change い→かった instead.
L5 N5
な-adjectives
na-adjectives
Quasi-noun adjectives
Form Use な before noun: 静かな町. Otherwise treated like nouns: 静かです. Negative: 静かじゃありません.
あの町は静かです。
Ano machi wa shizuka desu.
That town is quiet.
EN tip な-adj act like nouns until they modify another noun (then add な). Includes loanwords like ハンサム.
L5 N5
好き / 嫌い (like/dislike — な-adj)
suki / kirai
Like / dislike (uses が)
Form Object liked + が好き. Negative: 好きじゃありません.
犬が好きです。
Inu ga suki desu.
I like dogs.
EN tip 好き is a な-ADJECTIVE, not a verb. Object uses が, NOT を. 'I like X' = 'X is likable (to me).'
L5 N5
Counters (~まい ~ぽん ~こ etc.)
~mai / ~hon / ~ko
Object-specific counters
Form Number + counter. 紙 (paper) uses ~まい. 鉛筆 (long thin objects) uses ~ほん. General: ~つ (1-10).
りんごを三つください。
Ringo o mittsu kudasai.
Three apples, please.
EN tip Hundreds of counters exist. Start with つ (general), 個, 人, 本, 枚. Beginners default to ~つ when unsure.
L5 N5
~ましょう (let's)
~mashou
Let's do ~ (suggestion)
Form Verb stem (ます removed) + ましょう. Question form ~ましょうか? = 'Shall we?'
一緒に行きましょう。
Issho ni ikimashou.
Let's go together.
EN tip Very common — used in formal & casual settings. ~ましょうか? is polite for offering to do something for someone.
L6 N5
te-form (て形) ★
te-form
Connector form — joins clauses, makes requests, allows...
Form Rules vary by verb group. Group 1 (う-verbs): 書く→書いて, 飲む→飲んで, 立つ→立って. Group 2: drop る, add て. Irregular: する→して, 来る→きて.
ご飯を食べて、寝ます。
Gohan o tabete, nemasu.
I'll eat and then sleep.
EN tip ★ CRUCIAL FORM. The single biggest hurdle in early Japanese. Required for ~てください, ~ている, requests, permissions, prohibitions, and connecting clauses. Memorize the rules.
L6 N5
~から (reason)
~kara
Because ~
Form Reason clause + から + result clause. Always end the REASON with から.
寒いから、ジャケットを着ます。
Samui kara, jaketto o kimasu.
Because it's cold, I wear a jacket.
EN tip Opposite order from English 'X because Y'. Japanese: 'Y kara X'. Reason FIRST, then action.
L6 N5
~てください (please do)
~te kudasai
Please do ~ (polite request)
Form te-form + ください. Negative request: ~ないでください.
ここに座ってください。
Koko ni suwatte kudasai.
Please sit here.
EN tip Despite ください meaning 'give', this is a normal polite request, not begging. Use freely with strangers.
L6 N5
~てもいい / ~てはいけない
~temo ii / ~te wa ikenai
May ~ / Must not ~
Form te-form + も いい です = permission. te-form + は いけません = prohibition.
ここでタバコを吸ってもいいですか？
Koko de tabako o sutte mo ii desu ka?
May I smoke here?
EN tip Combines te-form with new grammar. ~ても is 'even if'. Critical for navigating Japanese politeness.
L6 N5
~ましょうか (shall I?)
~mashou ka
Shall I do ~ for you?
Form Verb stem + ましょうか. Offers help.
手伝いましょうか？
Tetsudaimashou ka?
Shall I help you?
EN tip Polite offer. Different from ~ませんか (would you like to?) which suggests doing together.

L7-8 · ~ている, particle が, short forms

日本語ユニバース · Globi Genki L1-12 · For native English speakers 4 / 5
L7 N5
Counting people 一人 二人...
hitori / futari / sannin
People counter
Form 1 and 2 irregular (hitori, futari). 3+: number + 人 (nin).
三人います。
Sannin imasu.
There are three people.
EN tip Memorize: hitori, futari, then sannin (3), yonin (4 — irregular: read 'yo' not 'shi'), gonin (5)...
L7 N5
Verb stem + に行く (purpose)
~ni iku
Go to do ~
Form Verb stem (ます removed) + に + 行く/来る/帰る.
買い物に行きます。
Kaimono ni ikimasu.
I'm going shopping.
EN tip に here is purpose, not location. The verb stem becomes a 'noun of action'.
L7 N5
~ている (progressive/state) ★
~te iru
Be doing ~ / be in state of ~
Form te-form + いる. Progressive (action ongoing) OR resultant state.
今、本を読んでいます。
Ima, hon o yonde imasu.
I am reading a book now.
EN tip Has TWO meanings: (1) ongoing action ('eating'), (2) result state ('married' = 結婚しています, 'know' = 知っています). The state meaning catches English speakers off guard.
L7 N5
te-form connecting sentences
te-form chaining
Doing X and then Y
Form te-form chains actions in sequence. The last verb carries the tense.
朝起きて、ご飯を食べて、出かけます。
Asa okite, gohan o tabete, dekakemasu.
I wake up, eat, then go out.
EN tip Time/sequence flows from first to last. Compare to from-/from-which in English.
L7 N5
Family terms (mine vs yours)
haha / okaasan
Humble vs respectful family
Form Own family (humble): 父 (chichi), 母 (haha), 兄 (ani), 姉 (ane). Other's family (respectful): お父さん, お母さん, お兄さん, お姉さん.
母は医者です。
Haha wa isha desu.
My mother is a doctor.
EN tip Strict in-group/out-group distinction. Calling YOUR mother お母さん to others sounds childish. Calling SOMEONE ELSE'S mother 母 is rude.
L8 N5
Particle が (subject marker)
~ga
Subject marker (new info / answer to questions)
Form Use が when (1) answering 'who/what?' questions, (2) introducing new info, (3) with existence verbs.
誰が来ましたか？— 田中さんが来ました。
Dare ga kimashita ka? Tanaka-san ga kimashita.
Who came? — Tanaka came.
EN tip が vs は is THE eternal pitfall. Rule of thumb: は marks WHAT the sentence is about (topic), が marks WHO/WHAT does the verb (subject, new info). With く 形容詞 like 好き, ALWAYS が.
L8 N5
何か / 何も
nanika / nanimo
Something / nothing
Form 何 + か = something (positive). 何 + も + negative verb = nothing.
何か食べますか？— 何も食べません。
Nanika tabemasu ka? Nanimo tabemasen.
Will you eat something? I won't eat anything.
EN tip Same pattern with 誰 (who): 誰か (someone) / 誰も (no one). The も version REQUIRES negative.
L8 N5
Short forms (普通体 / dictionary form)
futsuutai
Casual / dictionary form verbs
Form ます → dictionary form (行きます → 行く). Negative: ない (行きません → 行かない).
今日は学校に行く。
Kyou wa gakkou ni iku.
I'm going to school today.
EN tip Used with friends, family, in writing, and required for many grammar patterns. The 'real' form of the verb (dictionary entries use this).
L8 N5
Verb as modifier (~ている人)
modifying nouns with verbs
Relative clauses
Form Plain verb + noun. Verb describes the noun.
あそこにいる人は誰ですか？
Asoko ni iru hito wa dare desu ka?
Who is the person over there?
EN tip Japanese relative clauses come BEFORE the noun (opposite of English 'who/which/that' clauses). No relative pronoun needed.
L8 N5
Nominalizing with の / こと
verb + no / koto
Verb-ing as noun
Form Plain verb + の or こと turns verb into 'the act of ~ing'.
本を読むのが好きです。
Hon o yomu no ga suki desu.
I like reading books.
EN tip の and こと both nominalize verbs. Subtle preference rules — の for concrete actions, こと for abstract/general. Beginners can use の mostly.

L9-12 · Past, comparison, ~たい, ~んです

日本語ユニバース · Globi Genki L1-12 · For native English speakers 5 / 5
L9 N5
Plain past affirmative (~た)
~ta
Plain past tense
Form Same rules as te-form, but て → た / で → だ. 行く→行った, 食べる→食べた.
昨日、寿司を食べた。
Kinou, sushi o tabeta.
I ate sushi yesterday.
EN tip Mastering te-form gives you ta-form free. Just swap ending sound.
L9 N5
Plain past negative (~なかった)
~nakatta
Plain past negative
Form Plain negative ~ない → ~なかった.
昨日、食べなかった。
Kinou, tabenakatta.
I didn't eat yesterday.
EN tip い-adj. follow same pattern: 高くない → 高くなかった. The ない suffix conjugates like an い-adjective.
L9 N5
Qualifying nouns with clauses
relative clauses
Adjective clauses
Form Full clause (in plain form) + noun.
私が買った本
Watashi ga katta hon
The book I bought
EN tip Subject of relative clause uses が (not は). Plain form is required (no です/ます inside).
L9 N5
~から (because, plain form)
~kara
Because (with plain form)
Form Plain form clause + から. Common in casual speech.
雨が降ったから、行かなかった。
Ame ga futta kara, ikanakatta.
I didn't go because it rained.
EN tip Same から as before, but explicitly used with plain forms in casual speech.
L10 N5
Comparison: より / のほうが
~yori / ~no hou ga
X is more ~ than Y
Form Y より X のほう が ~ (X is more ~ than Y). Order: standard X-で is-more-than-Y.
犬より猫のほうが好きです。
Inu yori neko no hou ga suki desu.
I like cats more than dogs.
EN tip Word order can flip. Important: のほうが is the 'more' part, より is the 'than' part.
L10 N5
Superlative: ~の中で一番
~no naka de ichiban
The most ~ (within a group)
Form Group の中で + adjective + のは + thing (or 一番 + adj. directly).
果物の中でりんごが一番好きです。
Kudamono no naka de ringo ga ichiban suki desu.
Of all fruits, I like apples best.
EN tip 一番 literally means 'number one'. Use it as 'most' in superlatives.
L10 N5
Indefinite: どこかに / どこにも
dokoka ni / doko nimo
Somewhere / nowhere
Form Question word + か (some-) or + も (negative '-no-').
どこかに行きたい。 / どこにも行かない。
Dokoka ni ikitai. / Doko nimo ikanai.
I want to go somewhere. / I'm not going anywhere.
EN tip Same pattern works with 何 (something/nothing), 誰 (someone/no one), いつ (sometime/never).
L10 N5
~になる / ~くなる (become)
~ni naru / ~ku naru
Becomes ~
Form Noun + に + なる. い-adj. drop い + く + なる. な-adj. + に + なる.
暑くなりました。 / 先生になりたい。
Atsuku narimashita. / Sensei ni naritai.
It became hot. / I want to become a teacher.
EN tip なる expresses change of state. Different から する (do) which suggests deliberate action. なる = natural/inevitable change.
L10 N5
~つもりです (intention)
~tsumori desu
I plan to / intend to ~
Form Plain dictionary form + つもり + です. Negative: 動かないつもりです.
明日、大阪に行くつもりです。
Ashita, Oosaka ni iku tsumori desu.
I plan to go to Osaka tomorrow.
EN tip Stronger than just 'will' — implies a personal commitment or plan. Different from もうつもりはない (no intention).
L11 N5
~や~ (non-exhaustive 'and')
~ya ~
Things like X and Y (among others)
Form Noun や Noun (など). Implies more items exist beyond what's listed.
本やノートを買った。
Hon ya nooto o katta.
I bought books and notebooks (among other things).
EN tip と lists EVERYTHING. や lists SOME representative items. Subtle but important distinction in nuance.
L11 N5
~ことがある (experience)
~koto ga aru
Have ~ed before
Form Plain PAST form + こと が ある. Negative: ~ことがない.
日本に行ったことがあります。
Nihon ni itta koto ga arimasu.
I have been to Japan before.
EN tip Must use PAST form even though English uses present perfect ('have been'). Common error: using dictionary form.
L11 N5
~たい (want to do)
~tai
I want to ~
Form Verb stem (ます removed) + たい. Conjugates like い-adjective: ~たくない, ~たかった.
ラーメンが食べたい。
Raamen ga tabetai.
I want to eat ramen.
EN tip OBJECT particle changes: を often becomes が with ~たい (preference, not action). Only for FIRST person — using ~たい about others sounds presumptuous; use ~たがる instead.
L11 N5
~たり~たりする
~tari ~tari suru
Doing things like ~ and ~
Form Plain past + り + Plain past + り + する. Non-exhaustive list of activities.
週末は本を読んだり、寝たりします。
Shuumatsu wa hon o yondari, netari shimasu.
On weekends I do things like reading and sleeping.
EN tip Indicates SAMPLES of activities, not all of them. Compare to て-form chains which list ALL the activities in sequence.
L12 N5
~でしょう / ~だろう
~deshou / ~darou
Probably ~ / right?
Form Plain form + でしょう (formal) / だろう (casual). Rising intonation = seeking confirmation.
明日は雨でしょう。
Ashita wa ame deshou.
It will probably rain tomorrow.
EN tip Used by weather forecasters constantly. Different functions: (1) probability, (2) seeking agreement (rising tone).
L12 N5
~ほうがいい (you should)
~hou ga ii
It's better to ~
Form Plain PAST form + ほうがいい. Negative: ~ないほうがいい (better not to).
早く寝たほうがいいです。
Hayaku neta hou ga ii desu.
You should sleep early.
EN tip Often advice given to others. Past form is required for affirmative advice — strange to English speakers. Negative uses ない form.
L12 N5
~んです / ~のです (explanatory)
~n desu / ~no desu
It's because / the reason is ~
Form Plain form + ん (or の) + です. Provides context or explanation.
今、忙しいんです。
Ima, isogashii n desu.
It's because I'm busy now.
EN tip Adds emotional/explanatory weight. Sounds defensive or explanatory. Overuse can sound whiny. Listen to native speakers to learn nuance.
L12 N5
~ので (because, polite)
~node
Because (softer than から)
Form Plain form + ので. Sounds more polite/objective than から.
疲れているので、休みます。
Tsukarete iru node, yasumimasu.
Since I'm tired, I'll rest.
EN tip Used in formal/polite contexts (workplace requests). から sounds more assertive. な-adj/noun: use ~なので.
L12 N5
~なくちゃ いけない / ~なければ ならない
~nakucha ikenai / ~nakereba naranai
Must do ~
Form Drop い from ない, then add ~くちゃいけない (casual) or ~ければならない (formal).
宿題をしなくちゃいけない。
Shukudai o shinakucha ikenai.
I have to do homework.
EN tip Literally means 'if I don't do it, it won't do' — a double negative. Many shorter casual forms: ~ないと, ~なきゃ. Very common.
L12 N5
~すぎる (too much)
~sugiru
Too ~ / overdo ~
Form Verb stem + すぎる. い-adj drop い + すぎる. な-adj + すぎる.
食べすぎた。
Tabesugita.
I ate too much.
EN tip Conjugates as a regular る-verb: 食べすぎる → 食べすぎます → 食べすぎた. Indicates an undesirable excess.
